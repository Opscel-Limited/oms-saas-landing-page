BS 7671 Amendment 4, published as **BS 7671:2018+A4:2026**, is the current edition of the IET Wiring Regulations. The IET and BSI published it on **15 April 2026**, and the previous version (BS 7671:2018+A2:2022+A3:2024) was withdrawn six months later, on **15 October 2026**. For electricians, the change you'll notice first is on the EICR: only a C1 or C2 now makes a report unsatisfactory. Amendment 4 also adds a new chapter on stationary batteries, a new section on Power over Ethernet, and a major revision of medical locations. This guide covers what changed and what to do about it.

## What is BS 7671 Amendment 4, and when did it take effect?

Amendment 4 is an amendment to the 18th Edition, not a new edition. It folds in Amendment 3 (2024) and adds changes throughout the regulations. It's often called the "Orange Book".

The dates that matter:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 15 April 2026 | BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 published by the IET and BSI |
| 15 October 2026 | BS 7671:2018+A2:2022+A3:2024 withdrawn. A4 is the only current version |

According to NICEIC, new work **started on or after 15 October 2026** should be certified to Amendment 4. Work designed before that date can be completed and certified to the edition it was designed against, even if it finishes later. If a job straddles the date, record which edition it was designed to.

On the paperwork, Amendment 4 shows as "BS 7671 2026" in declarations, and NICEIC uses form reference **18.4** for A4 forms (18.3 was Amendment 3).

## What changed for EICRs?

This is the change that reaches the most electricians. Under Amendment 4, the observations on an Electrical Installation Condition Report are split into two groups:

- **C1** (danger present, immediate remedial action is necessary) and **C2** (potentially dangerous, urgent remedial action is necessary) **make the outcome unsatisfactory**.
- **C3** (improvement is recommended) and **FI** (further investigation is advised) **don't affect the overall outcome**.

Previously, an FI classification on its own made the installation unsatisfactory. Under A4 it doesn't. The FI wording has also changed: it no longer says "without delay".

| | Before A4 (A3:2024) | Amendment 4 (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Unsatisfactory | Unsatisfactory |
| C2 | Unsatisfactory | Unsatisfactory |
| C3 | Doesn't affect outcome | Doesn't affect outcome |
| FI | Unsatisfactory | Doesn't affect outcome |
| FI wording | Further investigation required without delay | Further investigation is advised |

FI doesn't mean "ignore it". It still needs writing up clearly so the client understands something should be looked at. But a report with only C3 and FI observations is now satisfactory. Our guide to [EICR observation codes](/blog/bs-7671-eicr-codes-explained) explains each code in more detail, and [What is an EICR?](/blog/what-is-an-eicr) covers the landlord side.

## What are the new battery storage rules?

Amendment 4 adds a new chapter covering **stationary secondary batteries** (NICEIC refers to it as Chapter 57). The IET says it responds to the rapid growth in energy storage, covering power conversion, bidirectional inverters, and fire risk and ventilation.

If you install home battery systems alongside solar PV or EV charging, this chapter now applies directly to that work. NICEIC notes there is **no** requirement in BS 7671 to fit an AFDD on a circuit supplying a battery energy storage system.

## What does the new Power over Ethernet section cover?

**Section 716** is new and covers Power over Ethernet (PoE), where data cabling also carries power to devices such as cameras, access points and lighting. The IET says connections should comply with **BS ISO/IEC 11801-1** and be rated for **750 mA per contact**.

As more lighting and building systems run on PoE, data cabling work increasingly falls within the scope of the Wiring Regulations.

## What else changed in Amendment 4?

- **Medical locations (Section 710)** has been significantly revised and aligned with IEC 60364-7-710:2021, including Group 0, 1 and 2 locations and requirements for supply redundancy in Group 2.
- **Functional earthing for ICT equipment** gets a new section on functional earthing and functional equipotential bonding (NICEIC cites Section 545).
- **PNB earthing** (protective neutral bonding, a form of TN-C-S) is now referenced on the new certificates and forms.
- **AFDD terminology** has been tightened. AFDDs are required on socket-outlet circuits rated up to 32 A in care homes, houses in multiple occupation, purpose-built student accommodation and **high-rise residential buildings**, defined by NICEIC as over 18 metres or more than six storeys.
- **Minor Works** forms use updated wording for end-to-end resistance (r1–r1, rn–rn and r2–r2).

## What do you need to do now?

1. **Use A4 forms for new work.** Any EIC, EICR or Minor Works for work started from 15 October 2026 should be on Amendment 4 model forms.
2. **Re-read your EICR outcomes.** Don't mark a report unsatisfactory because of an FI alone.
3. **Check your software and templates.** Certificates generated from old templates may still cite A2:2022 or A3:2024. Make sure the edition, form reference and observation wording are current.
4. **Update your knowledge.** If you install batteries, PoE or work in medical locations, read the new sections in full. Check with your awarding body for the current qualification on the 18th Edition.
5. **Explain the change to landlords.** Clients used to "FI means fail" will see more satisfactory reports. A short note on the report helps.
