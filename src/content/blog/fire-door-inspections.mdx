Opscel now handles fire door inspections from start to finish. Keep a register of the doors in each block, put every door on the floor plans, inspect them on the phone, and send the responsible person a report that shows each door's result on the plan.

It is built for the regular cycle of inspection and remedial work: communal doors every three months, flat entrance doors every year, and the remedials that come out of each round.

## The doors, once

Each fire door is one asset on the site's register, with its reference, location, function (communal, flat entrance or other) and rating (FD30 to FD120). Add them one at a time or import them from a spreadsheet.

The door's function sets how often it is due: every **3 months** for a communal door, every **12 months** for a flat entrance door and every **6 months** for anything else. Change it per door if a block needs something different.

## Floor plans with a pin for every door

Upload the plans for each level as an image or a PDF. From a PDF you tick the pages that are floor plans and they are added in one go, one floor per page. Then drag each door onto its floor, and Opscel numbers the pins.

Pins are coloured by each door's last result, so a glance at a floor shows where the problems are.

Doors not placed yet? Your engineers and site managers can place them from the phone as they walk the building: scan the door's tag, tap **Place on plan**, tap where the door is, and **Save pin**. **Scan next door** goes straight back to the camera, so you can work along a corridor.

## Inspecting on the phone

Book a job with the **Fire Door Inspection** service type and every door at the site arrives on the engineer's phone, ready to inspect. They can work down the list, or open the plan and tap a pin.

For each door the engineer records:

- **A checklist**: door, frame and wall, letterbox, glazing, hinges and ironmongery, gaps, strips and seals, the self-closer, alterations, whether it is wedged or held open, signage and other damage.
- **Gap readings** in millimetres at the hinge side, lock side, head, meeting stiles and threshold.
- **An outcome**: pass, remediate, replace leaf, replace doorset or not inspected, with a priority and remedial notes.
- **Up to three photos** of that door.

A shortcut fills the core checks for a door that is clearly fine, but it never answers the self-closer or the signage: those are always the engineer's call.

No access to a flat? The engineer records the reason and the steps taken, such as knocking twice and leaving a card. It is logged as an attempt, not a service, so the door stays due.

Everything works without signal, including the plans, photos and pins, and it all sends when the phone is back online.

## A report the responsible person can use

The completed sheet produces the **Fire Door Inspection Report**:

| Section | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Building | The responsible person, building height, premises type and whether Regulation 10 checks are likely to apply |
| Summary and door schedule | Every door with its checks, outcome and priority |
| Findings by door | The failed or advisory checks, notes and that door's photos |
| Action plan | The work for each door, by priority |
| No-access log | Each door that could not be inspected, why, and the steps taken |
| Floor plans | One page per floor, with numbered pins coloured by result and a key |

Every door that needs work also becomes a **defect** for the office, with the engineer's notes and the door's photos. Bundle them into a remedial quote in a few clicks.

## Your engineers make the call, not the software

Gap readings outside the usual range show as a warning to check against the door's own certificate, never as an automatic fail. A door is never failed automatically for missing seals or certification. The judgement on every door stays with the inspector.

## Who can use it

Fire door inspections are part of the **Business** plan, because the doors and floor plans live on the asset register.
