There isn't one "fire alarm certificate". **BS 5839-1:2025** sets out a separate certificate for each stage of a fire detection and alarm system's life: **design**, **installation**, **commissioning**, **acceptance**, **verification**, **inspection and servicing**, and **extension or modification**. Which one you issue depends on the work you did. When a building manager asks for "the fire alarm certificate", they usually mean the latest servicing certificate, or the full set from when the system was installed. This guide covers each certificate, which jobs need which, and who signs them.

## What are the fire alarm certificate types?

| Certificate | Issued when | Signed by |
|---|---|---|
| Design | The system design is complete, before installation | The organisation responsible for the design |
| Installation | The system has been installed to the design | The organisation responsible for the installation |
| Commissioning | The system has been tested and is working as designed | The organisation responsible for commissioning |
| Acceptance | The system is handed over to the client | The client or their representative accepting it |
| Verification | An optional independent check that the system matches its design and commissioning records | The verifying organisation |
| Inspection and servicing | Each routine service visit | The servicing engineer's organisation |
| Extension or modification | An existing system is extended or changed | The organisation that did the work |

Each certificate covers its own stage, so each one is signed by whoever took responsibility for that stage. If your company designed, installed and commissioned the system, you sign all three, but they remain three separate certificates.

## Which certificate do you issue for which job?

| Job | Certificates |
|---|---|
| New system, start to finish | Design, installation, commissioning, acceptance (plus verification if the client asks for it) |
| Installing someone else's design | Installation and commissioning, with the designer issuing the design certificate |
| Routine six-monthly service | Inspection and servicing |
| Adding detectors or a new zone | Extension or modification |
| Replacing a control panel | Extension or modification, with commissioning of the new panel |
| Updating panel firmware | Extension or modification. The 2025 revision treats a firmware update as a modification |

Under the 2025 edition, new work on an extension should comply with the current standard, even if the system it joins doesn't. Our guide to [fire alarm servicing under BS 5839-1:2025](/blog/fire-alarm-servicing-bs-5839-1-2025) covers the other changes for modifications and service visits.

## What does the acceptance certificate do?

The acceptance certificate is the handover. The client, or someone acting for them, signs to confirm they've received the system and its documentation. That usually includes the logbook, the as-fitted drawings, the zone plan and the operating instructions. It's also the point from which the six-monthly service cycle is counted.

Don't issue it without the client's signature. An acceptance certificate signed only by the installer doesn't show that anyone on the client side accepted the system.

## Is the verification certificate required?

Verification is an **optional** independent check that the installed system matches its design and commissioning records. It's usually requested by the client, an insurer or a consultant on larger or higher-risk projects. When it's done, the verifying organisation issues its own certificate.

## What changed for certificates with BS 5839-1:2025?

BS 5839-1:2025 took effect on **30 April 2025** and replaced the 2017 edition. NSI's transition guidance:

- A system **designed** to the 2017 edition, but not yet installed or commissioned, can keep a design certificate that refers to BS 5839-1:2017.
- **Installation, commissioning and acceptance** carried out under the 2025 recommendations should be certified to BS 5839-1:2025.

Other changes that affect paperwork:

- **Firmware updates are modifications**, and need a certificate.
- **All agreed variations** go in the logbook, not just "major" ones.
- The servicing certificate is based on the 2025 model form. If your templates still cite 2017, update them.

## Where does the electrical side fit?

BS 5839-1 points the electrical installation aspects of the system to **BS 7671**. The mains supply to the control panel is part of the electrical installation, so it's certified to the Wiring Regulations, separately from the fire alarm certificates. See [EIC vs EICR vs Minor Works](/blog/eic-vs-eicr-vs-minor-works-certificate) for which electrical certificate applies.

## Where does BAFE SP203-1 fit?

**BAFE SP203-1** is a third-party certification scheme for fire detection and alarm companies. It's split into modules (design, installation, commissioning and handover, maintenance, and verification), and a company is certified for the modules it carries out. Clients and insurers often ask for BAFE-registered contractors. The scheme expects the full certificate trail from BS 5839-1 for each stage you're certified for.
