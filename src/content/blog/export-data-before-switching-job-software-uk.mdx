Before switching job software, export your customers and sites, your certificates and invoices as PDFs, and your open jobs, then cancel last. Take the files while you can still log in, check that they open, and keep the old system running alongside the new one until you are sure nothing is missing.

Most contractors only think about this when something forces the issue: a price rise, a missing feature or an outage. The best time to check what you can take with you is before you need to.

## What should you export before switching job software?

Not everything is equally urgent. This is the order that protects you most:

| What | Why you need it | Usual export |
|---|---|---|
| Customers, sites and contacts | Running the business and booking repeat work | CSV or Excel |
| Certificates and reports (EIC, EICR, fire alarm, PAT) | Proof of work done, and customers ask for copies | PDF |
| Quotes and invoices | Disputes, queries and your accounts | PDF, plus CSV for accounts |
| Open jobs and recurring visits | Work already booked | CSV, or a printed list |
| Asset and service history | The next service or inspection depends on the last | CSV or PDF asset register |
| Photos and attachments | Evidence of the installation and any defects | Bulk download, if offered |
| Templates and price lists | Rebuilding them from scratch takes days | CSV, or copy and paste |

## How long should you keep certificates and job records?

There is no single rule, so here are the fixed points:

- **Landlord customers need copies.** Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, a landlord must keep a copy of an electrical report until the next inspection is due and give it to the next inspector. They will come to you if they have lost theirs.
- **Claims can arrive years later.** Many firms keep job and certificate records for at least six years, because six years is the usual limit for bringing a contract or negligence claim in England and Wales (Limitation Act 1980). Ask your insurer and your certification scheme what they expect.
- **Personal data should not be kept for ever.** Customer details are personal data under UK GDPR. Keep them while you need them, store exports securely and delete old copies.

## How do you get your data out?

1. **Find out what your plan can export.** Check the settings and the supplier's help pages before you tell anyone you are leaving.
2. **Export in priority order.** Customers and sites first, then certificates, invoices, open jobs and assets.
3. **Open a sample of every file.** Check the row counts, the dates and any names with accents or apostrophes. A file that will not open is not a backup.
4. **Ask for what you cannot self-serve.** If a type of data has no export button, ask the supplier in writing for the format and a date.
5. **Store it securely.** Use an encrypted drive or a protected cloud folder, not an email attachment.
6. **Read the cancellation terms.** Check the notice period and how long the supplier keeps your data after you leave.

## What do job software tools often struggle to export?

Tools differ a lot, so test yours instead of assuming. A common pattern is that customer lists export as a spreadsheet, while jobs, quotes and certificates only come out as PDFs, one at a time.

| Data | Often available as | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Customers and sites | Spreadsheet | Are contacts and multiple sites included? |
| Jobs and quotes | PDF or a basic list | Can I get them as data, not just documents? |
| Certificates | Individual PDFs | Can I download all of them in bulk? |
| Asset history | Sometimes a report | Do test readings and service dates come with it? |
| Photos | Per job | Is there a bulk download? |

Ask each question before you cancel, not after.

## Should you run the old and new systems side by side?

Yes, for a few weeks. Finish open jobs and issue their certificates in the old system, and start new jobs in the new one. Certificates you have already issued stay as PDFs. Do not reissue them.

Cutting over by customer group, or by engineer, keeps the risk small. Cancel the old system only when a full round of recurring visits has run cleanly in the new one.

You can import customers (with their sites and contacts), addresses, products, assets, jobs and service contracts from CSV or Excel files. There is a template for each type, and every import is validated, shown as a preview and confirmed before anything is saved, with an error report for any rows that fail.
