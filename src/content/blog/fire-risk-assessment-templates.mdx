Opscel's fire risk assessment now works the way your firm already assesses. You can write your own questions, give assessors suggested answers to tap instead of typing the same sentence again, and issue a report with a cover photo, photos on each action and an action plan at the back.

Two things changed together. The report itself is better, and the questions behind it are now yours to shape.

## A report that reads like a finished document

The fire risk assessment PDF has been rebuilt around what a client wants to see when it lands in their inbox.

- **A cover photo.** Pick the photo of the premises that goes on the front.
- **Photos on the actions.** Pick up to three of the job's photos for each action. Small thumbnails, labelled P1, P2 and so on, print under that action, and the full-size photos follow in the photo pages so the reader can match the two.
- **Severity and timescale on every action.** Each action carries a severity (Serious, Minor or Best practice by default) and a timescale (Immediate in 7 days, then 3, 6 or 12 months). The due date is worked out from the assessment date, so nobody does the sum by hand.
- **An action plan at the back.** The action plan now prints last, after the photographs: what was found, what to do, how serious it is, when it is due and which photos show it. It is the page the responsible person will actually use.

Reports already issued keep the layout they were issued with. Nothing about an old report changes.

## Your own question templates

Fire risk assessment questions are not one-size-fits-all. A block of flats, a care home and a small office do not need the same list, and most firms have a house style they would rather not retype.

Go to **Settings → Risk assessment templates**. You can start from Opscel's built-in questions or from a blank page, then change anything:

- **Sections and questions.** Add, reorder or remove them, and write each question so that Yes is the satisfactory answer.
- **Help text.** A line under the question for the assessor, such as "Check stairwells, landings and communal corridors".
- **Suggested answers.** For a question answered No or Unknown, write the comments you find yourself typing over and over.
- **A pre-filled action.** A suggested answer can carry a finding, a recommendation, a severity and a timescale, ready to drop into the action plan.

Each template can be tied to one or more service types. When a fire risk assessment is created for a job, Opscel uses the template that matches the job's service type, and falls back to your default template if none does. With no templates at all, everything works exactly as it did before, from the built-in questions.

## Suggested answers, where the assessor needs them

The assessor sees your suggested answers under a No or Unknown, on the office form and on the phone.

- **Tap a suggestion** and it is added to the comment on a new line. It never replaces what the assessor has already typed, and tapping the same one twice does not repeat it.
- **Tap "+ Add action"** and the pre-filled action is appended to the action plan, once per question.
- **Edit freely.** Everything a suggestion fills in is an ordinary field the assessor can change.

The point is speed and consistency, not removing judgement. The assessor still decides the answer, and still decides whether a suggestion fits what they are looking at.

## Editing a template never rewrites a report

When a fire risk assessment is created, it takes its own copy of the questions. If you improve a template next month, reports already started carry on with the questions they began with, and the report you issue to a client matches the questions the assessor actually answered.

Archiving a template stops it being offered for new reports. Existing reports keep their copy.

## Who can use it

Anyone who can edit settings in your business can build and change templates. Assessors do not need any extra access: they get the suggested answers on the reports they are already working on.

## Try it

Open **Settings → Risk assessment templates**, start from the built-in questions, and change a few. Then create a fire risk assessment on a job of the matching service type and see the suggestions appear under a No.

If you want the regulatory background first, our explainer on [fire risk assessments under the Fire Safety Order](/blog/fire-risk-assessment-uk) covers who needs one and what it must include, and the [certificates overview](/features/certificates) shows where the fire risk assessment sits alongside the rest of Opscel's compliance paperwork.

A note on scope: Opscel gives you the questions, the structure and the report. Competence, judgement and the conclusions remain the assessor's responsibility, and templates are a starting point you are expected to review for the premises in front of you.

Not to be confused with the risk assessments on a job: those are your [RAMS](/docs/rams), which protect your engineers while they work. A fire risk assessment is the report you produce about a client's premises.
